0 BugBook: Why guidelines and standardization in insect research? 

Research on the use of insects as a source of food and feed is experiencing significant growth, as it may contribute to the transition from linear agricultural practices to a more sustainable circular system (van Huis and Oonincx, 2017). For instance, a search in Google Scholar for articles containing “Tenebrio molitor” or “Hermetia illucens” in the title reveals an increase in publications from 115 in 2014 to 961 in 2023 (Figure 1), compared to a total of only 534 publications from 2004 to 2013. This growth rate surpasses the overall increase in scientific publications over a similar period (47% from 2016 to 2022; Hanson et al., 2024), indicating a rising interest and engagement in this emerging field.

The increase in research enhances our understanding of insects as sources of food and feed, and it is anticipated that this trend will persist into the future. However, although not quantitatively substantiated, it is likely that the number of experimental methods employed is increasing at a similar rate. This proliferation of diverse methodologies may hinder the comparability of results and complicate future meta-analyses. As with other livestock species in the past, it is evident that this emerging field could benefit from the establishment of guidelines and standardization for both experimental procedures and publications. Such guidelines could facilitate the onboarding of new researchers as well as senior researchers, from other scientific fields, by reducing the learning curve. This approach would minimize the wastage of valuable resources on unsuccessful or irrelevant experiments and enhance the quality of research from the outset. Additionally, standardization would improve the comparability of results across different studies and over time, similar to practices established for other livestock. Ultimately, this not only benefits researchers but also supports the industry and governmental bodies as it will improve benchmarking and improved science driven regulations.

In 2019, during the EAAP conference in Ghent, a working group focused on the “standardization of methods, parameters, and terminology in insect rearing” was established, prompted by the publication of Bosch et al. (2020), despite the presence of only 492 articles at that time. The next significant development occurred during the OECD (Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development) workshop on the standardization of research methodologies, held as part of the Insects to Feed the World conference in Quebec City, Canada, in 2022. This event initiated the conceptual groundwork for a methodology and guidelines book, inspired by the successful COLOSS BEEBOOK (Facchini et al., 2023), which was initiated over a decade ago and has since expanded to three volumes, downloaded over 100 000 times. This concept was further refined in the subsequent EAAP conferences (2022-2023), culminating in the current Special Issue: BugBook.

This Special Issue represents the culmination of a five-year effort involving over 100 researchers from 18 countries. However, it is hoped that this is not the concluding chapter, but rather the beginning of new research endeavors, fostering constructive discussions and leading to the establishment of standardized methodologies. Ultimately, the aim is for this initiative to achieve a level of success comparable to that of its inspirational antecedent.
